Accessibility

Updated October 13, 2026.

Matro should work for everyone in the home, including people who see poorly, don't see at all, need large text or get motion sick. This page says what the app does for that, and what we know it doesn't handle yet.

VoiceOver

  • Everything in the app has labels, in Swedish and English. A row is read as a whole, for example “Milk, 2 liters, not checked off” or “Dinner, meatballs, Anna is cooking, 6 servings”.
  • Headings for every part and every day of the week, so you can jump between them with the rotor.
  • Amounts, servings, ratings and the servings dial are changed by swiping up or down.
  • What is otherwise done with a gesture is also available as an action: move a dinner to another day, move an ingredient up or down, answer the dishes in Weekly picks.
  • The app tells you when something happens: an item is added or checked off, a dinner is moved, everyone matches on a dish, a timer is done or a change couldn't be saved.
  • In Cook now the step is read out with its number, and timers tell you when they start, when one minute is left and when they are done.
  • The Basket game can be played: swipe up or down to aim and double-tap to drop. The app tells you what the piece lands on and how many points you have.

Voice Control

The buttons are named after what they say, so you can say for example “Tap Add” or “Tap Cook now”. Everything done with a gesture also has a button or an action.

Larger text

All text follows the text size in Settings, all the way up to the largest accessibility sizes. Rows and buttons stack when they don't fit side by side. A few parts, like the cards in Weekly picks and the big numbers, stop growing at a certain size so that everything fits. You can read them in a larger format by pressing and holding, or with VoiceOver.

Contrast and appearance

  • The app follows the phone's appearance, dark or light, and you can choose one under Settings → Appearance.
  • Text has a contrast of at least 4.5:1 against its background, as WCAG 2.2 AA asks. This is checked by tests and measured on real screenshots.
  • With Increase Contrast the text gets brighter and the colored light in the background fainter.
  • With Reduce Transparency the glass in the app becomes fully opaque.

Color and motion

  • Color is never the only sign: what is selected or done also has a checkmark, a strikethrough or text. With Differentiate Without Color checkmarks are shown even where the color would otherwise do.
  • With Reduce Motion things fade in instead of flying, cards don't swipe away and there is no confetti or flashes.

Widgets and the Lock Screen

Timers, cooking and the shopping list on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island are read out by VoiceOver, for example “Pasta, 4 minutes left” or “Shopping: 9 left”.

What we know isn't good yet

  • The Basket is a game you watch. With VoiceOver it can be played, but it isn't as much fun.
  • The dish photos have no description beyond the dish's name.
  • Most of it has been tested with Apple's accessibility audit and in the simulator. If you find something that doesn't work, we'd love to hear about it.

Get in touch

Is something not working for you, or is something missing? Write to team.app.consortium{at}gmail{dot}com. Tell us which setting you use, and we'll fix it as soon as we can.
